# Industry Benchmarks (Default, Overridable)

Published benchmarks for the same platform vary enormously depending on methodology
(follower-based vs. view-based engagement, organic vs. blended reach, industry vertical,
account size tier). Two credible 2026 reports can disagree by 10x on the "same" number.
Rather than presenting a single figure as ground truth, this file picks one dated,
sourced default per platform and metric. Always disclose the source and date next to
the benchmark in the report — never present it as an unqualified fact.

**Override rule:** if the user supplies their own benchmark figures (internal targets,
a specific industry report they want used, a prior year's own performance as the bar),
use those instead and say so in the glossary. Otherwise fall back to the defaults below.

**Small-base rule:** benchmarks are noisy at low sample sizes. If the account's own
follower count is under 1,000 or the metric's base (e.g. impressions) is under 1,000,
note that benchmark comparison is unreliable at this scale rather than declaring
over/under-performance.

## Facebook (Page)

| Metric | Default benchmark | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement rate per post, all industries (interactions ÷ followers) | ~0.15% | Quid/Rival IQ 2026 Social Media Industry Benchmark Report |
| Organic engagement rate by industry (interactions ÷ followers) | 0.02%–0.23% depending on industry | Rival IQ 2026 |
| Engagement rate (interactions ÷ reach) | No sourced default | — |

Rival IQ divides interactions by **follower count** ("Engagement rate is calculated
based on all these interactions divided by total follower count", Rival IQ benchmark
report methodology). An earlier version of this file labelled the ~0.15% figure as
interactions ÷ reach; it is not. Facebook exports usually give reach but not total
followers, and a reach-based rate runs many times higher than a follower-based one
(a 1.9% reach-based rate against this 0.15% looked like a 13x outperformance). So
when only a reach-based rate can be computed, report it without a benchmark verdict,
or show the benchmark labelled "follower-based, not comparable", never as a
like-for-like bar.
| CTR (link clicks ÷ impressions) | ~0.90% average, 1.5–2.5% good, >2.5% excellent | Industry compilation, 2025–2026 |

Facebook organic reach and engagement are structurally low platform-wide in 2026 —
a rate that looks poor by Instagram or TikTok standards may be entirely normal for Facebook.

## Instagram

| Metric | Default benchmark | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement rate (interactions ÷ followers) | ~0.30%–0.48% (declining trend since 2023) | Quid 2026 (0.30%) / Socialinsider 2026 (0.48%) |
| CTR | ~0.22% average, 0.5–1% good, >1% excellent | Industry compilation, 2025–2026 |

Note the declining multi-year trend (from ~17% under different follower-tier/small-account
methodologies down to under 1% in broad brand-account studies) — always state whether a
rate looks low against the *current* benchmark, not an outdated one from a prior year's report.

## TikTok

| Metric | Default benchmark | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Engagement rate, follower-based (interactions ÷ followers) | ~2.0%–2.7% | Quid 2026 / Socialinsider 2026 |
| Engagement rate, view-based (interactions ÷ views) | ~4.2% | Socialinsider 2026 |
| CTR | ~0.30% average, 0.5–1% good, >1% excellent | Industry compilation, 2025–2026 |

TikTok's algorithm distributes content well beyond the follower base, which is why
follower-based and view-based engagement rates diverge so much. State which denominator
was used any time a TikTok engagement rate is reported.

## YouTube

| Metric | Default benchmark | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions CTR | 2–10% typical range, 4–6% average for most channels | YouTube Help Center guidance, corroborated across 2026 industry sources |
| Average view duration | >40% of video length = strong, >50% = excellent, <30% = weak (hook/pacing issue) | Industry compilation, 2026 |
| Engagement rate (likes+comments ÷ views) | ~2.3% (down from ~3.7% YoY) | Metricool 2026 Social Media Study |

YouTube CTR is highly channel-size dependent: sub-1,000-subscriber channels often see
6–10% CTR because impressions skew toward subscribers; channels above 100,000 subscribers
typically see 3–5% as impressions skew toward colder audiences. Note channel size when
judging CTR against benchmark.

## Refreshing these numbers

These are snapshot defaults as of 2026 and will go stale. If the person building or
running this skill has a more current benchmark report, replace the table above rather
than layering a correction on top of it.
