# Dated tells: vocabulary by era, retired patterns, per-model habits

Read this before treating any word list or typographic habit as current. Tells change with each model generation, so a word that was a giveaway in 2023 may say only that the text is from 2023 — or nothing at all, since human writing has been absorbing these habits since 2022.

Two rules follow. First, date the text before you flag its vocabulary: a 2024 draft full of 2024 words is unremarkable. Second, flag the literal word rather than its synonyms, and check the sense — "underscore" can mean a typographic mark or a film score, and "key" is an ordinary noun.

## Vocabulary by model era

From Wikipedia's *Signs of AI writing*, which tracks which words peaked with which generation. One or two of these in a piece is coincidence. Many, repeatedly, in post-2022 text is among the stronger tells in the catalogue.

| Era | Words that peaked then |
|---|---|
| 2023 – mid-2024 (GPT-4) | additionally, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, garner, intricate/intricacies, interplay, key, landscape, meticulous/meticulously, pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant |
| Mid-2024 – mid-2025 (GPT-4o) | align with, bolstered, crucial, emphasizing, enhance, enduring, fostering, highlighting, pivotal, showcasing, underscore, vibrant |
| Mid-2025 onward (GPT-5) | emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing — plus the notability and attribution vocabulary in tell 8 |

Notes that matter when applying this:

- *Delve* was the famous 2023 tell and dropped off sharply through 2025. Finding it now dates a text more than it identifies one.
- The vocabulary has shifted from ornamental adjectives toward notability-justifying phrasing. An audit that only looks for *tapestry* and *vibrant* will miss current output entirely.
- Several supposedly retired words — *intricate*, *interplay*, *tapestry*, *testament* — are still reported in current output. Treat the table as a guide to peaks, not to extinction.
- Grok is idiosyncratic: pseudo-scientific vocabulary (*causal*, *empirical*, *correlate*) and still-heavy *underscore* as of 2026.

## Retired or fading patterns

Useful for dating a text or auditing an archive, not for judging current output.

- **Didactic disclaimers** (late 2022–2024): "it's important to note", "it's worth noting", "may vary by jurisdiction". Common enough in early models that OpenAI's own GPT-4 documentation used them as examples of partial refusals.
- **Section summaries and tidy conclusions**: "In summary", "Overall", "In conclusion", plus a restatement at the end of each paragraph. Wikipedia files these as characteristic of older models. They are also what school essays, executive summaries, and legal memos teach, so in those genres they are conventions rather than tells.
- **Prompt refusals**: an apology and an "as an AI language model" disclaimer before an alternative.
- **Abrupt cut-offs**: text stopping mid-generation at a token limit. Also caused by a mangled copy-paste.
- **Outdated access dates** on citations, older than the edit that added them. Newer tools rarely do this, and legitimate old access dates exist.
- **Elegant variation**: conspicuous synonym cycling for one referent across a passage — protagonist, main character, central figure — caused by older models' repetition penalties. Careful: writers taught to avoid repetition, including many educated outside English-speaking countries, do this naturally.

## Per-model habits

Models have distinct idiolects, so "sounds like AI" usually means "sounds like the model I use". Worth checking which one the user has in mind.

- **ChatGPT and Grok** lean harder on broader-context framing — the trend, the debate, the legacy.
- **Gemini and Claude** run more concise by default.
- **Em dashes**: a July 2026 study found Claude the only current model using them more than professional human writers; ChatGPT used fewer by then, after deliberate tuning.
- **Curly quotes**: typical of ChatGPT and DeepSeek output, not of Claude or Gemini.
- **Claude** has its own cluster of habits, catalogued as tell 24 in `tells.md`.

## What this means for an audit

State the currency of what you flag. "Four instances of 2023-era vocabulary" and "four instances of current notability padding" are different findings about different texts, and a reader who can't tell them apart will over-read the first one.
