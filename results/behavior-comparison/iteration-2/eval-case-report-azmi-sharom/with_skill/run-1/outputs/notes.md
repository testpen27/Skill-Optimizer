**Unverified or conflicting**
1. I did not read the judgment itself. The kehakiman.gov.my PDF returned 404. All holdings and quotes come from secondary reports. Section 3(1)(a) and the specific tendency limb on which Azmi was charged are unconfirmed.
2. No dissent or separate judgment was found. One search summary said the decision was unanimous, but I did not check it. The report says "none found in the sources consulted".
3. No obiter dicta were identified with confidence. I did not label any passage as obiter. The report records that Yuneswaran called Sivarasa's "reasonable" reading obiter.
4. The commencement date of the Sedition (Amendment) Act 2015 conflicts across sources. Wikipedia and ex-IGP Musa Hassan say gazetted 4 June 2015. ARTICLE 19 (October 2015) says gazette was still awaited. Whether the 2015 amendment removed the "Government" limb of s. 3(1)(a) also conflicts (Lawyers for Liberty and Musa Hassan say yes, Wikipedia says no).
5. Penalties: the original is 3 years or RM5,000. The report only says that the 2015 amendments "raised" penalties, on the strength of the UN High Commissioner as reported by ARTICLE 19. I did not verify the new figures.
6. Singapore: the repeal Act passed on 5 October 2021. A commencement date of 2 November 2022 appears only on Wikipedia and is not stated in the report. Singapore's ICCPR status is from memory and tagged [to verify].
7. India: the abeyance (11 May 2022) and the BNS s. 152 date (1 July 2024) come from search summaries. *Kedar Nath Singh* (1962) is from memory and tagged. The UK Coroners and Justice Act 2009 s. 73 is from memory and tagged.
8. The Heidy Quah February 2026 ruling (CMA s. 233) comes only from the repo's status snapshot and is tagged [to verify].
9. The Sivarasa citation uses the AmerBon form [2010] 2 AMR 301. The Mat Shuhaimi report citation was not verified ([2017] 1 AMR 15 appears in AmerBon's footnote). Its date of 25 November 2016 is from Malay Mail.
10. Sources differ on how the trial ended. Columbia says the AG withdrew the charges under Art 145(3) on 12 February 2016, and one search summary said "acquitted". The report follows the AG withdrawal.
11. The claim that Nordin Salleh is distinguishable (it concerned a state constitution) is my own reading, and the report flags it as such. The "structural" Art 162 reasoning is partly my gloss on the quoted passages.
