# Official academic literature matrices: source notes

Read on 2026-11-23 for the `research-matrix` skill. Scope is academic
literature review only. Excluded on purpose: SWOT, competitor and marketing
comparison, risk, decision and any other matrix. Each entry below was read on
the page named; nothing is from memory.

## 1. Matrix families found

| Family | Rows | Columns | Official source read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Review matrix (Garrard, Matrix Method)** | one source per row, ordered by year | fixed first three: authors/title/journal, year, purpose; then headings chosen per review (methodological, content-specific, geographical) | Duquesne University Gumberg Library (`guides.library.duq.edu/matrix`, `/organize`, `/columns`); Brandeis University Writing Program handout; VCU Libraries; ECU Laupus Library |
| **Literature matrix (source characteristics)** | one source per row | University of Michigan list of trackable characteristics: year, authors, source title, publication name, citation, source type, abstract, geographic location, keywords, purpose/objectives, research questions/hypotheses, theoretical framework, methodology, sample/population, main findings, limitations, future research directions, notes | University of Michigan Library (`guides.lib.umich.edu`, Literature Reviews, Documenting your Search) |
| **LRM with THEY SAY / I SAY** | one source per row | THEY SAY: author/date, main ideas, theoretical/conceptual framework, methods, results and analysis, conclusions. I SAY: comments (your analysis), future research implications, professional practice implications | San Jose State University Writing Center handout |
| **Literature matrix (AMA, health)** | one source per row | author and date, theoretical/conceptual framework, research question/hypothesis, methodology, analysis and results, conclusions, implications for future research, implications for practice, notes; "fill in directly after reading" | Liberty University Libraries (`libguides.liberty.edu`, AMA Writing Guide), citing Garrard |
| **Synthesis matrix** | one theme/main idea per row | one source per column (or the reverse); each cell holds that source's point on the theme, with page numbers; gaps in the grid point to gaps in the literature | NC State Writing and Speaking Tutorial Services handout (hosted by FIU Writing Center); Johns Hopkins Sheridan Libraries; Williams College Libraries |
| **Concept matrix** | one article per row | one concept per column, marked where the article covers it; extended with a unit of analysis (organizational, group, individual). Concept-centric, not author-centric | Webster and Watson (2002), MIS Quarterly 26(2), xiii-xxiii; adapted from Salipante, Notz and Bigelow (1982) |
| **Characteristics of included studies** | one study per row | participants, interventions and comparators, outcomes, study design; from a piloted data collection form; duplicate extraction | Cochrane Handbook ch. 5 and ch. 9; Cochrane MECIR C43-C51 |

## 2. Rules the sources agree on

- A literature review synthesises; it is not a summary of each paper
  (ECU, Liberty, NC State, Webster and Watson).
- Order sources by date to see change over time (Duquesne, Brandeis).
- Do not add a column whose answer is the same for every study because it is
  already in the research question (Duquesne, Brandeis).
- Do not fill the matrix before reading the articles; purpose in particular
  can need a full read (Duquesne, Brandeis).
- Fill the row straight after reading each work (Liberty).
- Keep what the authors said apart from your own analysis: THEY SAY / I SAY
  (SJSU); a notes column for quotations and thoughts (Liberty, Michigan).
- Record page numbers with synthesis-matrix cells (NC State).
- Empty cells in a synthesis matrix are findings: they mark gaps (NC State).
- Columns are chosen to serve the research question; there is no single
  correct set (SJSU, Duquesne, Liberty).
- Webster and Watson: tables must add value by categorising, not merely
  list articles; completeness means not limiting to one journal set, method
  or region; stop when no new concepts appear.
- Cochrane: pilot the extraction form, extract in duplicate where it matters.

## 3. What the baseline skill already covers, and what it does not

Baseline (extracted from `pre-research`): one row per paper; columns
Title, Author(s) and Year, Quartile, Method, Access, Introduction,
Methodology, Results, Discussion, Theoretical Framework; a synthesis
paragraph; PDF output.

Compared with the sources:

- Present: author/year, methodology, results, conclusions-like discussion,
  theoretical framework.
- Absent from the baseline but standard in the sources: purpose or research
  question as its own column; sample or population; geography or context;
  limitations; implications for future research; implications for practice; a
  notes column; a separate "my analysis" (I SAY) apart from what the paper
  reports; page numbers; year ordering rule; concept or theme columns
  (synthesis and concept matrices).
- Quartile, Access and Method labels are tooling conveniences with no basis
  in the official matrices. They are a design choice to keep or drop.

## 4. Sources not read in full

Garrard's book (Health Sciences Literature Review Made Easy: The Matrix
Method, Jones and Bartlett) was reached through library guides that quote it,
not read directly, so book-only claims are not used here. Salipante et al.
(1982) is known only through Webster and Watson's citation.
